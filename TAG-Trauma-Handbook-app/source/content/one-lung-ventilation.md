## Key Principles

- Requirement for OLV is extremely rare in acute major trauma patients. Occasionally intra-thoracic access is surgically difficult or overwhelming lung leak from trachea-bronchial injury necessitates this.
- DLT and OLV are more frequent in patients coming for urgent/ semi-elective VATS surgery.

## Indications for DLT & Lung Isolation

- For the acute setting there is very little clinical scenario that cannot be managed by placement of a **LEFT** sided DLT. Thereafter the non-injured side may be preferentially ventilated. Selective right main bronchus intubation can be done with a single lumen ETT in extremis.

## Double Lumen Tubes

![Double lumen tubes: indications, size and depth](fig/olv-dlt.jpg)

**Do you need a DLT?**

- Surgical access requires it?
- Broncho-pleural fistula?
- Pulmonary haemorrhage?
- Purulent lung pathology?

**DLT size?**

| Female | Male |
|---|---|
| <1.5m = 35F | <1.6m = 37F |
| <1.6m = 37F | <1.7m = 39F |
| >1.6m = 39F | >1.7m = 41F |

**Depth?**

- 1.7-1.8m = 29cm @ incisors
- +/- 1cm for every 10cm difference
- e.g. 1.6m = 28cm

## Placing the DLT

![DLT insertion sequence](fig/olv-insertion.jpg)

1. Turn the AP curve of the DLT to face laterally and direct the bronchial lumen through the vocal cords; then pause
2. Seek assistance from the ODP to remove the stylet
3. Turn DLT to neutral and advance to correct depth in a single smooth movement
4. Join the DLT via the Y-connector to the anaesthetic circuit

> Over correcting by 90<sup>O</sup> (tube at 9o’clock position) is usually helpful at this point before advancing to the correct depth. At depth, turn tube back to neutral.

## Checking Isolation

**Bag ventilate, check that you are in the trachea:**
Bilateral chest expansion, Et CO<sub>2</sub>, misting? If yes, then with the assistant holding the DLT secure at the correct depth. Next, inflate the tracheal cuff until leak disappears at the mouth (using 10ml syringe). Bag ventilate and auscultate.

**Now check side-to side isolation:**

1. Clamp tracheal lumen on the Y-connector in a downwards direction, and opens venting cap
2. Inflate bronchial cuff (in 0.5ml increments, max 2-3ml) until step change decrease in leak/no leak felt
3. Chest expansion/breath sounds heard only on L
4. Switch the clamp to the bronchial lumen, open cap, close tracheal lumen cap
5. Chest expansion/breath sounds should now only be evident on the contralateral side

![Checking isolation; bronchoscopic view of the carina](fig/olv-photos.jpg)

**Bronchoscopic check: supine & reconfirm in the lateral position:**

- Pass bronchoscope down tracheal lumen to view bifurcation
- Confirm anterior/posterior trachea and left vs. right bronchus
- Optimal to see a small rim of blue bronchial cuff at the carina
- Take a mental snap shot of this view for a quick assessment of subsequent change in the tube’s position

## Troubleshooting DLTs

![Troubleshooting DLTs: one lung ventilation and malposition](fig/olv-trouble.jpg)

### One Lung Ventilation

- Lung protective: ~ 3 ml/kg on OLV
- PEEP 5-8 cmH2O is helpful in most cases
- Stay on FiO<sub>2</sub> 1.0 until 15-20 min after commencing OLV, then wean to 0.6
- Deflate the bronchial cuff when OLV is no longer required

### HYPOXAEMIA: SpO<sub>2</sub> < 88%

*(as a guide, this may vary case-to-case)*

1. **Immediate action**
    - Hand ventilate with 100% O<sub>2</sub>. This allows you to check:
        - The circuit is patent
        - The compliance – is the upper lobe obstructed?
    - Suction
    - Recruitment manoeuvre – helpful after lateral positioning & suction
2. **Ventilated lung (V/Q mismatch)**
    - Increase FiO<sub>2</sub> to 0.6 – 1.0
    - Increase minute volume
    - Bronchoscope to check upper lobe orifice is not obstructed
    - Improve perfusion: fluids, vasopressors
3. **Non-ventilated lung (shunt)**
    - Bronchoscope to check upper lobe
    - Intermittently ventilate – discuss with the surgical team
    - Clamp pulmonary artery

### Malposition

- Tube down incorrect side i.e. L-sided DLT in RIGHT main bronchus
- Bronchial cuff herniation because DLT position is not deep enough
- DLT too deep

### Ongoing management

- Ventilated lung: Utilise frequent recruitment manoeuvers, adjust PEEP and allow permissive hypercapnoea if physiology will allow.
- Non-ventilated lung: Passive insufflation of oxygen or CPAP (1-5cmH<sub>2</sub>O) may be administered via a Water’s Circuit from the external oxygen supply of the anaesthetic machine if significant hypoxaemia.
