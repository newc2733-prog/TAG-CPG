## Considerations in Elderly Trauma

- Adults > 65 years-old; may also apply to younger, but frail patients.
- Patients are at a higher risk of morbidity and mortality.
- Ageing, comorbidity and medications affect presentation and physiology.

> **INJURY MECHANISM:**<br>**Minor mechanisms cause major trauma to the head, spine, chest and pelvis.**
>
> Falls from standing constitute the most common cause of trauma and related deaths.
>
> **The trauma may be the result of an underlying medical event.**

## Specific Physiology & Comorbidity

**Chest:** Trauma is higher mortality/morbidity due to poor functional reserve, increased chest wall rigidity, loss of muscle mass, reduced gas exchange and altered central responses to hypoxia and hypercarbia.

**CVS:** Standard “abnormal” parameters of shock physiology are not always applicable:

- **Systolic BP < 110 – suspicious for shock,** particularly in a poorly controlled hypertensive.
- **HR > 90 suspicious for shock,** especially if beta-blocked.
- Shock is less well tolerated, and hypotension may be exaggerated.
- Significant CVS disease in 50-65% of patients.

**Musculoskeletal:** Long lie is common, leading to dehydration, hypothermia and renal failure; check CK. Increased risk of **hypothermia**.

**Neurological:** Cerebral atrophy increases potential space in skull → bleeding may go unnoticed. Repeated assessment and lower threshold for CT head. At risk of delirium.

**Practical:** Do not lose glasses, dentures or hearing aids! They are difficult to replace and jeopardise return to function, early rehab and delay discharge.

## Perioperative Management Key Principles

### Pre-op

**Trauma and medical assessment:**

- Review imaging for additional injuries.
- Review mobilisation and stability of spine and pelvis.
- Assess and optimise any immediate medical issues or comorbidities.
- Consider risk assessment tool: NSQIP, P-Possum, SORT, Nottingham hip score.

**Medications:**

- **Has anticoagulation been reversed if indicated?** Consider early anticoagulant reversal for patients with known or suspected haemorrhage.

**Early MDT involvement:**

- RLH has a **TPOPS Service** (Trauma & Perioperative Older Persons Service). They routinely see trauma patients >65 years during rounds on Mon/Wed/Fri.
- TPOPS are available for urgent input on **Dect Phone 45703**.
- If unavailable, consider the on-call medical registrar.

**Treatment escalation plan:**

- Documented decisions for CPR and level of escalation.
- Ensure patient and family involvement
- If **Anaesthesia + DNACPR**, DNACPR is normally reversed for immediately reversible causes. (Discuss at time of consent).

### Intra-op

**Type of anaesthesia:**

- Less important than care taken in maintaining homeostatic physiology.
- **Arm-brain time is prolonged, flush drugs and be patient, in order to avoid overdose.**
- Regional anaesthesia, without GA or deep sedation may enhance recovery/reduce complications.
- Consider early regional blocks/catheters, e.g. for rib fractures, lower limb, neck of femur fracture.

**Monitoring/Drips/Tubes/Practical:**

- Low threshold for arterial line and depth of anaesthesia monitoring.
- Monitor temperature and warm actively.
- Urinary catheters should be avoided unless necessary (increased risk of UTI, delirium and increased length of stay).
- Higher risk for pressure areas: meticulous padding.
- Avoid sedative medications, or those that can precipitate delirium e.g. midazolam, clonidine, atropine, high dose ketamine, cyclizine.

### Post-op

- Lower threshold for level 2 admission; 12 D trauma unit for complex trauma.
- Ensure regular analgesics (pain can often go undertreated), antiemetics, and laxatives are prescribed.
- Ensure patients are eating, drinking and moving.

## UNSTABLE C-Spine

Hard collar should be changed for soft Miami J/similar once injury confirmed.

Patients unable to tolerate C-spine immobilisation (e.g. dementia) should be managed with a ‘minimal movement’ approach:

- Comfort optimisation with proper fitting collar
- Assessment and management of contributors to delirium
- Assessment of risks/benefits of immobilization and sedation

## Poly-pharmacy

Most routine medications can continue, beware of oral hypoglycaemics and antihypertensives if low BP, or anticipated major bleeding.

| Medication | Consideration |
|---|---|
| Beta blockers | May obtund/ mask response to injury |
| ACEi/ ARB’s | May worsen shock or reduce effectiveness of therapy |
| Steroids | May require replacement and affect response to trauma |
| Anticholinergics | May cause agitation or confusion (cyclizine, ketamine) |
| Opioids/ BZO’s | May have exaggerated effects – dose with caution |
| Anti-Parkinson | Omission may worsen symptoms |

## Delirium

Delirium: clinical condition characterised by acute and fluctuant course of disturbed consciousness and cognitive function. Sleep disturbance or hallucinations may be present. Exists as hypo-/hyper-active or both.

Up to 50% of patients develop delirium after trauma/surgery. 4AT is a screening test that helps detect delirium.
