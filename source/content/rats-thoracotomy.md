## Definition

A thoracotomy is indicated for patients in extremis allows operator:

- To relieve cardiac tamponade (either confirmed with imaging or clinically suspected)
- To manage exsanguinating haemorrhage and where internal cardiac massage and clamping of the descending aorta is required

Specifically, these patients are in imminent circulatory arrest from cardiac tamponade or exsanguination despite maximal volume filling or are already in circulatory arrest with external massage ongoing and < 15 min since loss of vital signs. Outcomes are far better if performed before cardiac arrest occurs, regardless of location and if performed for cardiac tamponade.

A thoracotomy can be performed by HEMS Doctors, Trauma Surgeons and some ED or TAG Consultants who have undergone defined training. Thoracotomy may occur in Resus (Bay 8) or preferentially Theatre.

## Contraindications

- Prolonged down-time (>15mins) +- CPR performed
- Multiple injuries inconsistent with survival
- Blunt trauma exsanguination

## Anaesthesia for thoracotomy

- Minimal / none if in cardiac arrest, minimal if severe shock.
- Titrate small amounts of an IV agent, eg Fentanyl / Ketamine, as needed and minimal MAC (if at all).

## Techniques

- Clamshell thoracotomy - allows access to the lungs, major vessels including the aorta, the mediastinum and the heart within the pericardial sac.
- Pericardiotomy - for direct management of penetrating cardiac injury and evacuation of pericardial clot.
- Aortic clamp - for management of exsanguinating sub-diaphragmatic injury and to optimise myocardial and cerebral perfusion (increased afterload).
- Anaesthetic - VCV, F<sub>i</sub>O<sub>2</sub> 1.0, minimum MAC, Ketamine / Fentanyl
- Aggressively re-warm.
- Beware loss of aortic occlusion during transfer from trolley to table.

## Post-procedure care

- Transfer to theatre for further damage control surgery / resuscitation.
- Debrief with team.
- Clinical Governance procedures will follow.
- Analgesia - consider regional catheters sited by Surgeons for ‘well’ patients likely to be extubated within 24hrs, eg isolated tamponade with no cardiac arrest.
