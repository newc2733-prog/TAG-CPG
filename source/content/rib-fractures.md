## Key Principles

Multimodal opioid sparing analgesia with an appropriate regional anaesthetic is the cornerstone of rib fracture treatment and there is evidence to support its use to reduce pain scores and minimise morbidity from respiratory complications. Good analgesia ensures patient compliance with physiotherapy and appropriate respiratory support. Patients should be risk stratified, cohorted and managed at an appropriate level of care. Surgical fixation may be considered in selected cases and is a multidisciplinary decision coordinated by the trauma surgeons.

## Initial Management

**Analgesia + VTE to begin in ED**

**Single shot Serratus Anterior Block** (< 6 hours after initial imaging)

- Consider in all patients (inc. lateral and posterior fractures)
- Monitor observations post block for 30 minutes

### Multimodal Analgesia

| Tier | |
|---|---|
| **Tier 1 (regular)** | Oral paracetamol +/- ibuprofen; PRN oramorph or oxycodone |
| **Tier 2** | Consider PCA (1<sup>st</sup> line morphine, 2<sup>nd</sup> oxycodone, 3<sup>rd</sup> fentanyl) |
| **Tier 3** | Contact pain team/TAG for regional block if STUMBL > 16 |

> **Analgesia is adequate when:**
>
> - The patient can take deep breaths and cough,
> - Comfortable mobilising in bed, pain score ≤ 4/10

## Regional Anaesthesia Catheters

Patients should be considered for placement of an indwelling LA catheter within 24h of admission.

- All patients in pain or with worsening respiratory function despite conventional strategies/PCA
- All patients deemed high risk (STUMBL score ≥16)
- Patients with a flail segment

### CHOICE OF BLOCK:

| Injury | Block |
|---|---|
| Unilateral chest wall injuries | Erector Spinae Plane catheter or PVB catheter (depending on skillset) |
| Unilateral chest wall injuries with no access to the patient’s back | Serratus Anterior Plane catheter |
| Bilateral chest wall injuries or extensive (>5) unilateral fractures | Thoracic epidural |

## Epidural Analgesia

- Patient must be accepted to ACCU prior to siting catheter.
- Epidurals should usually be placed in awake patients unless there are exceptional circumstances. An MDT discussion between ICU Consultant, Trauma Consultant and TAG Consultant should determine if this is in the patient’s best interest.
- These patients are at risk of haemodynamic collapse due to fluid restriction and profound sympathetic block.
- It is a minimum safety requirement to have ODP assistance and AAGBI monitoring.
- It is prudent to have 2 anaesthetists where possible. One to titrate IV analgesia and pressor and the other to do the procedure.
- Site epidural at a midpoint of injury or slightly cephalad.
- These patients are in severe pain, the shorter the procedure, the better for the patient, therefore ensure all kit is prepared and assembled prior to positioning.
- Fentanyl or Ketamine (titrated at 5-10mg boluses) can be used for positioning.
- Give a test dose (5-10 mls of 0.125% bupivacaine or levobupivacaine and wait ten minutes for lack of appreciable response (leg weakness, drop in blood pressure)).
- Then load with further 5-10 ml aliquots at ten minute intervals until adequate analgesia.
- Run epidural at a rate appropriate to number of levels being covered. i.e. 8-14 ml/hour at maximum levels. Use 0.1% bupivacaine or 0.125% levobupivacaine.
- Some patients may still need combination with a PCA.

### Serratus Plane Block

- These fascial plane blocks are ideal for anterior and lateral fractures particularly for immobilised patients with spinal precautions.
- All patients with rib fractures should be offered a single shot serratus plane block within 6 hours of admission. This can be done by the ED team if they are appropriately trained to do so.
- There are very few contraindications.
- An epidural catheter or any other catheter kit may be used.

### Erector Spinae Block

- Appropriate for posterior, lateral and anterior fractures.
- May have a place where contraindication to other block options exists.
- Current evidence suggests it is a relatively safe intervention with low complication rates.
