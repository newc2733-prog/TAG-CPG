## Key Principles

- Assume difficult intubation (positioning, airway soiling, anatomical disruption).
- A cuffed tube in the trachea is the gold standard airway.
- An anticipated difficult airway is ideally managed in theatre.
- Reversal and wake up is rarely an option.
- It is unusual to perform awake fibreoptic intubation in acute trauma. Indications for intubation, patient acuity, and time often limit its use.
- Videolaryngoscopy (VL) is useful in documenting airway injury (e.g. laryngeal tears)

## Maintaining C-Spine Immobilisation

- Assume all facial trauma patients have a C-spine injury and minimise movement
- Remove C-Spine collar prior to intubation
- Delegate and brief trauma team member to perform MILS
- If the patient is agitated, do not restrain the head to immobilise the neck

## Pre-hospital placement of SGA by HEMS

- To be regarded as an anticipated difficult airway
- Jointly decide with the Resus team if definitive ETT required
- Plan to secure ETT in theatre whenever possible
- Check all the kit fits and slides easily – lignocaine spray will help this

### KIT & SIZES

- An Aintree catheter will pass down an IGEL size 3
- Smallest ETT to pass over the Aintree is a 6.5 ETT (will require lubrication)
- Use a catheter mount with a bronchoscopy port to maintain oxygenation
- Our typical Ambu-scope (Ambu aScope 4 Broncho Regular 5.0/2.2) will pass down an Aintree
- Insert the Aintree in the correct orientation in order to assess the depth correctly

### THINGS TO NOTE:

- Use a laryngoscope to advance the ETT over the Aintree it is also an opportunity to visualise the larynx and identify foreign bodies and potential grade of intubation
- Discuss with ACCU and document an airway plan for this patient on CRS

<li>Having prepared the fibrescope (FS) and camera system, lubricate the outer surfaces of both the Aintree Intubation Catheter (AIC) and FS. Preload AIC onto FS and secure with tape. <li>The SAD should be immobilised by an assistant. Introduce FS with loaded AIC through top port of swivel connector into the SAD lumen.</li>
<li>Sequentially visualise SAD aperture bars (if present), glottis, tracheal rings and finally carina as the FS passes caudally. Never advance beyond carina.</li>
<li>Note depth of AIC. With assistant immobilising SAD and the operator maintaining the position of AIC, the FS alone is withdrawn after removal of securing tape.</li>
<li>Briefly disconnect SAD (along with swivel connector) from anaesthetic circuit, deflate cuff (if present) and start withdrawing SAD (along with swivel connector), applying counter pressure on AIC to prevent movement. ## Facial bleeding and airway soiling

### Preparation

- If the patient is awake allow them to position themselves (e.g. sitting up, leaning forward) prior to induction. Preoxygenate as much as possible.
- Manage the airway on a trolley which can be tipped head down if necessary
- Lateral positioning is likely to be difficult in a trauma patient
- Ensure you have 2 x working suctions prior to intubation

### Airway Plan

- RSI with cricoid, bougie and direct laryngoscopy should be plan A
- Blood and vomit can obscure the image from VL and fibreoptic scopes. Consider having a spare VL blade and/or something to clean the blade with.
- Supraglottic airways can be considered in the case of a failed intubation, but these should be a temporising measure and do not protect the airway
- Have a low threshold to move to front of neck access
- Extensive facial trauma makes the face and mandible more mobile. Disruption of the upper airway can be disorientating. However, the majority of facial trauma patients are straightforward to intubate

### Aspiration Management

- Record in the notes any obvious airway soiling or aspiration
- Liquid soiling can be managed by irrigation of the trachea with saline (5-10ml) and endo tracheal suction
- Aspiration of solids (e.g. teeth) need interventional bronchoscopy.

## MaxFax trauma airway considerations

### Packing of facial bleeding

- Ongoing facial bleeding can be slowed by anatomical reduction of the facial fractures

This will be led by the trauma team. It is important you know the procedure and assist when required. The order of steps is crucial in preventing further anatomical distortion

1. Secure ETT as standard
2. Insert bilateral epistats along the base of the nasal cavity. Do not inflate.
3. Insert dental blocks either side of the ETT between the molars
4. Secure the cervical collar to brace the mandible
5. Inflate the small posterior balloon with 10ml Saline (inflate both simultaneously)
6. Inflate the middle balloon with 20-30ml Saline (inflate both simultaneously)
