## Terminology

Traumatic spinal cord injury (SCI) occurs when there is a disturbance to normal sensory, motor, or autonomic neurological function following a traumatic injury.

- The **sensory level** is defined as the most caudal dermatome in which both pinprick and light touch sensation are normal.
- The **motor level** is defined as the most caudal myotome with intact innervation, below which a motor deficit exists. Because of muscle poly-innervation, the most caudal muscle to have at least grade 3 power is considered to be fully innervated.
- The **zone of partial preservation** refers to the most caudal dermatome and myotome beyond the motor and sensory level that has some limited preserved function.

## Anatomical and Clinical SCI Syndromes

- **Conus medullaris syndrome**, or injury of the conus and lumbar nerve roots within the spinal canal. Usually results in areflexic bladder, bowel, and lower extremities.

![Spinal cord injury infographic: types of incomplete spinal cord injuries (apparelyzed.com)](fig/sci-infographic.jpg)

- **Cauda equina syndrome,** or injury between the conus and the lumbosacral nerve roots within the spinal canal, also results in areflexic bladder, bowel, and lower limbs.
- **SCIWORA** - Spinal Cord Injury Without Radiographic Abnormalities. Elasticity of the juvenile spine renders the spinal cord vulnerable to injury without vertebral column disruption. More common in children < 8 years old.

## Spinal Cord Injury Management Principles

- Advanced Trauma Call/ Code Red
- Prepare drugs for RSI and Resuscitation
- Primary Survey and Imaging as directed by the Trauma Team Leader (TTL)

### C-Spine Control

- Ensure padded head rolls + tape & log rolling for all movements
- Nurse patients flat, tilt bed 15-20<sup>o</sup> head up if tolerated
- Early use of padded collar (Miami J)

### Airway & Breathing

- Beware of vomiting in CT, consider ondansetron early
- Intubation: refer to Emergency Department RSI CPG
- Decompress the stomach with an NGT
- Avoid Suxamethonium > 72 hours post injury – risk of hyperkalaemia

### Circulation

- Hypotension = bleeding until proven otherwise
- Bradyarrhythmias (especially during intubation) and other arrhythmias are common
- Early arterial line and CVC
- Vagal episodes possible on rolling movement

### Disability

- ED should document neurology (ASIA chart)
- Maintain normothermia, normoglycemia
- Imaging as per TTL/Neurosurgeons
- Multimodal analgesia

> **Steroid therapy**
>
> - Not routinely recommended
> - May be considered by treating neurosurgical consultant
>
> Remember about bladder management (Foley catheter), thromboprophylaxis, GIT protection (PPIs, laxatives), skin protection (patients prone to pressure sores)
