## Definition

In a planned setting, awake tracheal intubation has a high success rate and low-risk profile in the management of predicted difficult airways. This is due to the maintenance of spontaneous ventilation, airway tone and patient cooperation. Trauma patients may have difficult airways including difficult facemask ventilation due to C-spine immobilisation precautions, being un-fasted, airway disruption, disrupted physiology and the urgency involved. AFOI is infrequently used but is a valuable skillset to maintain.

## Procedure

- **Oxygenate** - use HFNO unless contraindicated.
- **Topicalise** - Lidocaine 4% to oropharynx, tonsillar pillars, base of tongue, hypopharynx. If nasal intubation planned use co-phenylcaine as a vasoconstrictor.
- **Consider Sedation** - Usually Remifentanil.
- **Perform** - Confirm tube position (visual + EtCO<sub>2</sub>) before induction.

## Topical agents

- Varied practice, at the discretion of the Anaesthetist, options available:
- Xylocaine (Lignocaine spray)
- 4% Lignocaine (drawn up into a syringe and administered via a Mucosal Atomization Device (MAD))
- Otrivine (Xylometazoline ) (Vasoconstrictor)
- Lidocaine 5% / Phenylephrine 0.5%
- Instillagel

## Tips and tricks

- Maximum dose of lidocaine is 9mg.kg<sup>-1</sup> for topicalisation
- Anaesthesia should only be induced once two point check confirmed (visual confirmation of tube through cords + EtCO<sub>2</sub>)
- Sedation is not a substitute for proper topicalisation
- Supplemental oxygen should always be administered
- Logistics are complex for AFOI - strongly consider moving to Theatre
