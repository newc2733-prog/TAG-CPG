## General Principles

- **Assume a full stomach.** All intubations should be an RSI. Cricoid pressure is not mandated and is at the discretion of the Anaesthetist.
- **Manual in-line immobilisation** should be provided (after removal of cervical collar) where there are concerns for cervical spine injury. It may be appropriate to only apply this after induction of anaesthesia.
- **Assume hypovolaemia and unstable physiology.** Appropriately resuscitate this physiology prior to induction of anaesthesia using vascular access, blood products and drug therapy as necessary. Appropriately titrate induction drugs and have vasopressors prepared.
- **Assume difficult intubation** (positioning, airway soiling, anatomical disruption) and optimise all ergonomic elements to make first attempt the best attempt.
- **RSI may be conducted in the ED or in theatre** (including for difficult airway). Decisions are based on the circumstances and urgency and are at the discretion of the Anaesthetist.
- **Checklists:** TAG encourages the use of a checklist to ensure all equipment is present and all team members share the same mental model.

## Indications for ED RSI

- Actual or impending airway compromise
- Ventilatory failure
- Unconsciousness/ moribund/ peri-arrest
- Agitated head injury or intoxication
- Humanitarian (e.g., high analgesia requirements, burns, etc)

> **In these circumstances, once committed to advanced airway management, there is no option to abandon this. An airway must then be achieved.**

## Drugs

| | Awake | Shocked & Drowsy | Unconscious or Peri-arrest |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Fentanyl** | 1-2 mcg/kg | 0 mcg/kg | 0 |
| **Ketamine** | 2 mg/kg | 1 mg/kg | 0 |
| **Rocuronium** | 100mg | 100mg | 100mg |
| **Propofol Maintenance** | 10–30 ml/hr | 0–15 ml/hr | 0 |
| **Ongoing Fentanyl boluses** | 50-100mcg | 25–50mcg | 25–50 mcg |

Manage hypotension with balanced blood product transfusion

Vasopressors acceptable to treat side effects of anaesthesia drugs or when fully volume resuscitated and patient exhibits vasoplegia

## Plan D – Front Of Neck Access (FONA)

![Can't Intubate, Can't Oxygenate (CICO) in critically ill adults](fig/rsi-fona.jpg)

**CALL FOR HELP** – Declare "Can't Intubate, Can't Oxygenate"

**Plan D: Front Of Neck Airway: FONA**

- Extend neck
- Ensure neuromuscular blockade
- Continue rescue oxygenation
- Exclude oxygen failure and blocked circuit

### Scalpel cricothyroidotomy

Equipment: 1. Scalpel (wide blade e.g. number 10 or 20) 2. Bougie (≤ 14 French gauge) 3. Tube (cuffed 5.0-6.0mm ID)

Laryngeal handshake to identify cricothyroid membrane

**Palpable cricothyroid membrane**

- Transverse stab incision through cricothyroid membrane
- Turn blade through 90° (sharp edge towards the feet)
- Slide Coudé tip of bougie along blade into trachea
- Railroad lubricated cuffed tube into trachea
- Inflate cuff, ventilate and confirm position with capnography
- Secure tube

**Impalpable cricothyroid membrane**

- Make a large midline vertical incision
- Blunt dissection with fingers to separate tissues
- Identify and stabilise the larynx
- Proceed with technique for palpable cricothyroid membrane as above

**Trained expert only** – Other FONA techniques: Non-scalpel cricothyroidotomy; Percutaneous tracheostomy; Surgical tracheostomy

### Post-FONA care and follow up

- Tracheal suction
- Recruitment manoeuvre (if haemodynamically stable)
- Chest X-ray
- Monitor for complications
- Surgical review of FONA site
- Agree airway plan with senior clinicians
- Document and complete airway alert
