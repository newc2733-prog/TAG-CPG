## Definition

Extra Corporeal Membrane Oxygenation (ECMO) consists of a large bore drainage cannula, a pump, a membrane oxygenator and a large bore return cannula. It can be set up for Veno-Venous (VV ECMO) support for severe respiratory failure or Veno-Arterial (VA ECMO) for cardiac and respiratory support.

The ECMO circuit allows for easy administration of very large volumes of blood products as well as precise temperature control, warming and cooling.

In trauma, VA ECMO has an evolving use for **refractory cardiogenic shock.**

## Indications

- Post ROSC for traumatic cardiac arrest.
- Early intra-operative cardiogenic shock not responding to normal resuscitation.
- Trauma Induced Secondary Cardiac Injury (TISCI) in the ICU phase of care: May be evidenced by pulmonary artery parameters.
- Following pneumonectomy for severe pulmonary trauma (high risk of acute right heart failure).
- Warming in profound hypothermia & hypothermic cardiac arrest.
- Uncontrollable hyperkalaemia leading to cardiac instability.

## Considerations

- Modern heparin-bonded circuits do not mandate systemic anticoagulation.
- VV ECMO for respiratory failure is a nationally commissioned service, for RLH - this is provided by Papworth Hospital and has a **separate referral pathway** to the Bart’s Trauma Shock Call.
- Time to initiation: Shock Call and ECMO team mobilisation will **take at least 1 hour**.
- Cannulation: Usually via femoral vein and contralateral femoral artery, though it can be done by other central routes or into central vessels when the patient has an open chest.
- Patient location: ECMO patients will usually be transferred to Bart’s Hospital for ongoing care
