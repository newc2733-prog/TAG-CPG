## General Information

- Pregnancy itself is an independent predictor for mortality after trauma. (1.6x increased risk for death)
- Rate of fetal loss approaches 40-50% in life threatening trauma.
- Obstetric and neonatal teams may be unfamiliar with the Emergency Department or Theatre environments and current major trauma clinical practice.

## Relevant Anatomy

| Gestation | |
|---|---|
| **<12 weeks** | Uterus is intra-pelvic organ |
| **±20 weeks** | Fundus is at height of the umbilicus |
| **34 – 36 weeks** | Fundus is at height of costal margin. Bowel is pushed cephalad. Uterus is thin walled and elastic. Inelastic placenta makes it vulnerable to shear forces at utero-placental interface (abruption). |
| **>36 weeks** | Foetal descent into pelvis, head usually presenting. Pelvic fracture may result in foetal skull fracture. |

## Imaging in Pregnant Trauma

### Ultrasound

- FAST is appropriate during initial resuscitation but is limited by advancing pregnancy.
- Do not delay a CT (if indicated) for a FAST.
- FAST is poor at detection of abruption.

### Conventional Radiology & CT scans

- **Do Not avoid** conventional X-rays or CT in pregnant patients.
- The dose of ionizing radiation used in trauma evaluation is well below dosage thresholds known to cause harm to the foetus.
- Iodinated contrast is safe in pregnancy.
- MRI is safe at any stage of pregnancy, but gadolinium should only be used if considered absolutely necessary.

## Foetal Assessment

- Assessment of a viable foetus **(>23 weeks)** should be initiated immediately following or in parallel with the physical examination of the stabilised mother.
- Identification of impending foetal injury, death from utero-placental compromise or trauma-related complications (abruption, pre-term delivery, spontaneous rupture of membranes, foetal injuries, foetal demise) is the aim.
- In-utero resuscitation includes **supplemental O<sub>2</sub>, IV volume replacement, left lateral positioning.**

## Pregnant Trauma Resuscitation

> **IN ALL DECISION MAKING, THE NEEDS OF THE MOTHER HAVE PRIMACY**
>
> **Best treatment for the foetus is optimal resuscitation of the mother & early foetal assessment**

## Peri-mortem Caesarean Section

| Indications | Contraindications | Important Details |
|---|---|---|
| • Gestation > 20 weeks<br>• No ROSC after 4 minutes of correctly performed CPR | • Gestation < 20 weeks<br>• Arrest > 30 minutes<br>• Early ROSC | • Do not delay based on lack of access to kit – scalpel is all that is required<br>• Vertical skin and horizontal uterine incisions are quickest |
