## Definition

The Pulmonary Artery Catheter (PAC) is a multi-lumen, balloon tipped catheter floated through the right heart to the pulmonary artery, it facilitates advanced haemodynamic assessment

![](fig/rats-pac-photo.jpg)

## Indications in trauma

- Complex, multifactorial hemodynamic instability with suspected cardiogenic component
- ECMO anticipated or initiated
- Less invasive monitoring inadequate for haemodynamic assessment

## Insertion

The PAC is inserted through an introducer, an introducer sheath with a valved port for the line. A trauma line (MAC line) includes an introducer sheath but alternative wide bore access must be available. Ideally it should be inserted into the RIJ, although left subclavian, right subclavian and LIJ sites are alternative options (in order of preference).

1. Sterile field around new/pre-existing sheath, operator gowned & gloved
2. Open PAC to sterile field, maintain curvature
3. 1.5ml syringe attached to red port, check balloon, passive deflation
4. 3-way taps attached to blue & yellow lumens
5. Bionector to other lumens, flush & check all
6. Connect CVP line to blue lumen, PA line to yellow lumen
7. Insert PAC through sterile cover & haemostasis valve
8. Curvature pointing to patient’s left if RIJ
9. Connect sterile cover to haemostasis valve

## Floating the PAC

1. Ensure PA waveform corresponds to distal, yellow lumen on PAC
2. Insert to 16-20cm, confirm RA waveform
3. Inflate balloon with 1.5ml air via red port and lock
4. Advance smoothly until RV then PA waveforms seen (~40-50cm if RIJ)
5. Gently advance a little further until waveform flattens (PAOP)
6. Note depth, deflate balloon and withdraw 2-3cm
7. Ensure PA waveform returns
8. Connect CCO cable (after testing: see below) & SvO2 cable
9. XR in ICU: catheter tip in West Zone 3, below LA

## Risks

- Risk of arrhythmias
    - Advance carefully, monitor closely
    - If occur stop, deflate balloon, withdraw catheter, wait, manage as needed
- Catheter looping in RV
    - >15cm from RV without PA trace
    - Deflate balloon, withdraw, retry
    - Too slow insertion allows PAC to heat up and soften
    - Rotate PAC to re-align curvature
- Do not leave PAC wedged - risk of PA rupture, infarct
- Always deflate balloon to withdraw - risk of valve damage
- Wedge waveform present without balloon inflation - tip too peripheral, risk of infarct
- Keep wedges to a minimum (10-15s)
- Do not use saline in balloon - deflation may not be possible
