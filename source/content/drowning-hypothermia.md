## Terminology

> **Drowning** is the process of experiencing respiratory impairment due to submersion or immersion in liquid.

- The following terms should **not** be used to categorise drowning patients and events: near, wet, dry, active, passive, salt-water, freshwater, or secondary.
- The distinction between salt water and freshwater drowning is no longer considered clinically relevant.
- If the person is rescued and the process of drowning is interrupted, this is termed a **non-fatal drowning**.
- If the person dies as a result of drowning, this is termed a **fatal drowning**.
- Any submersion or immersion incident without evidence of respiratory impairment should be considered a **water rescue** and not a drowning.
- **Hypothermia** is the pathological state in which the core body temperature falls below **35<sup>o</sup>C.**

| Grade | Core temperature |
|---|---|
| Mild | 32 – 35<sup>o</sup>C |
| Moderate | 28 – 32<sup>o</sup>C |
| Severe | 20 – 28<sup>o</sup>C |
| Profound | < 20<sup>o</sup>C |

## Pathophysiology

- Drowning begins with breath-holding which may be followed by a period of laryngospasm secondary to the presence of liquid in the oro-pharynx. This is associated with hypercarbia, hypoxaemia, and acidosis.
- Hypercarbia stimulates respiration, leading to active respiratory movements. The laryngeal spasm abates due to hypoxia and the victim inhales liquid.
- Fluid aspiration initiates an acute lung injury (ALI). Inhaled toxins including chlorine, pollutants, and particulate material may further contribute to pulmonary injury.
- In non-fatal drowning the clinical picture is hypoxaemia secondary to ALI/ARDS.
- Hydrostatic pressure of water on the body increases venous return and cardiac output. Loss of this pressure effect when exiting the water causes venous pooling and circulatory collapse. Patients should be extricated in a horizontal position.
- Vomiting and aspiration is a frequent complication.
- Volume and electrolyte disturbances may further contribute to instability.

> Full neurologic recovery rarely happens if > 10 minutes of normothermic submersion or > 40 minutes of hypothermic submersion.

- Rapid onset hypothermia is protective. However, in the UK temperatures low enough to achieve this even in mid-winter are unlikely.
- Water is thermally neutral (± 33<sup>o</sup>C). Most patients will drown in water at a temperature lower than this, thus concomitant hypothermia is common in drowning.
- Reversal of hypothermia is paramount in initial resuscitation of a drowning patient.
- Many ALS interventions are ineffective with low core body temperatures, including pacing, atropine and defibrillation. Follow RESUS guidelines in hypothermia.

## Key Principles of Relevance

- There is a **high incidence of associated injuries** in victims of drowning. **Head and cervical spine** injuries are particularly common in patients rescued from shallow water.
    - Treatment of a toddler drowning (<5 years) should focus on airway/ breathing management (low risk of spinal or associated trauma).
    - Adults & teenagers have the highest occurrence of spinal injuries (higher mechanisms of injury and increased risk factors).

> Patients who are asymptomatic and have no evidence of respiratory compromise (no CXR changes or hypoxia on ABG) after 6 – 8 hours may be safely discharged home.
> **All symptomatic patients should be admitted for observation.**

- Antiarrhythmics should be withheld for core temperatures of **< 30 °C**.

## Drowning Management Principles

- Advanced Trauma Call
- Prepare to treat hypothermia: Bair hugger, Inditherm, Fluid Warmer, **ECMO Alert**
- Primary Survey and Imaging as directed by the Trauma Team Leader (TTL)

| | |
|---|---|
| **C-Spine Control** | |
| Low risk of C-spine injury (~0.5%) unless: history of high energy mechanism or other traumatic injuries | Type of water is irrelevant |
| **Airway and Breathing** | |
| Early intubation if obtunded, respiratory failure or risk of aspiration<br>RSI ± MILS | Lung Protective Ventilation<br>Decompress the stomach via **NG** Tube |
| **Circulation** | |
| Treat Cardiac Arrest as per ALS/APLS<br>Arrhythmias are common<br>Arterial line/CVC context specific<br>If < 30<sup>o</sup>C: max 3x defibrillation attempts and hold cardiac arrest drugs | **Active External Re-warming \***<br>Warm environment, remove wet clothes, Bair Hugger & blankets, Inditherm mattress<br>**Active Internal Re-warming**<br>Warmed IV infusions, RRT, warm cavity irrigation, ECMO |
| **Disability** | |
| Neuroprotective measures<br>Correct Hypothermia to 32-34<sup>o</sup>C in arrest<br>Prevent Hyperthermia | No evidence for routine antibiotics or steroids |

> **\* In order to reduce ‘core temperature after-drop’ the trunk should be re-warmed BEFORE the extremities.**
>
> **After-drop**: Cold, acidaemic blood from vasoconstricted extremities returns to core circulation causing drop in temperature and pH, with simultaneous peripheral vasodilation from re-warming (causing precipitous hypotension).
